Respite, in three steps

How respite coordination works here.

The Discord post is a starting point — not the paperwork. All formal respite still goes through your licensing worker and the department.

01

Post the need

Dates, number of kids, general ages, and anything a caregiver would need to know. Keep names and case details out of the post.

02

Match in the thread

A nearby family replies. Move to direct messages to swap phone numbers and addresses.

03

Loop in your worker

Any formal respite arrangement needs your licensing worker's sign-off. Discord just helps you find the right family first.

Ground rules

Three rules. Read them once.

These exist to protect children, families, and the server itself. Breaking them isn't a slap on the wrist — it can jeopardize an actual case.

1

Never share confidential case or family information.

No names, case numbers, medical details, or identifying information about children or their biological families. Ever. Not in public channels, not in DMs, not in jest.

2

Case concerns go to your worker, not the server.

If you're having a problem with a case, a department, or a specific worker, that conversation belongs with the worker and the department. This server is not a place to air those grievances.

3

Keep it general and helpful.

This space is for general support, questions about fostering and foster care, and reaching out to other families to share items like furniture. It's mutual aid, not a marketplace or a complaint box.

For CFSD & agency partners

The case for letting your families know this exists.

If you supervise foster homes, place children, or carry a caseload — here's what this server is, how it protects confidentiality, and exactly what we are and aren't asking of you.

Partner Brief · MT Foster Parents Discord

Supported foster parents stay. Staying foster parents mean stable placements, fewer disruptions, and lower recruiting and licensing costs. MT Foster Parents is an invite-only, moderated peer community that provides that support at no cost to the department — organized by region, governed by strict confidentiality rules, and designed to lighten caseworker load rather than add to it.

Established Montana foster-care nonprofits — Child Bridge and Montana Foster Closet — already participate directly in the server.

01 Why this serves the department's goals

Foster home retention

Isolation and burnout are the quiet reasons families stop fostering. A peer network that answers at 10 p.m. is retention support the department never has to staff or fund.

Placement stability

When a caregiver hits a wall, access to peer respite can be the difference between a planned short break and an unplanned disruption.

Fewer repetitive inquiries

"Where do I get a crib?" and "Which providers take Medicaid?" get answered by peers in minutes — leaving caseworker time for casework.

Faster resource matching

A child needs a car seat this weekend. A family across town has one in the garage. What used to take a week of referrals takes an hour.

A safer alternative to informal groups

Families already network on unmoderated social media. This is the governed version: invite-only, rule-bound, moderated, and visible to the department if it chooses to be.

Region-organized, not scattered

Regional roles and channels mean local needs surface locally — useful signal for resource development in each area.

02 How confidentiality is protected

  • Rule 1 prohibits sharing any confidential case or family information — names, case numbers, medical or identifying details — in any channel. Moderators remove violations and coach members.
  • Rule 2 explicitly routes concerns about cases, the department, or individual workers back to the worker and the department. The server is not a grievance channel, by design.
  • Invite-only membership. Families join through a worker or moderator referral; the Foster Parent role is granted after verification.
  • Region roles keep local activity local and limit channel visibility to the appropriate families.
  • Not publicly searchable. Discord servers aren't indexed or crawlable the way public groups are.
  • Workers are welcome to hold a CPS worker role for visibility, with no obligation to participate.

03 Common concerns, answered directly

"What if a family posts case details anyway?"
Rule 1 is stated at onboarding, restated in #welcome-and-rules, and enforced by moderators. Violations are removed and the member is coached or removed.
"This will turn into a complaint board about my agency."
Rule 2 exists specifically to prevent that. Grievances about cases, the department, or workers are explicitly off-topic and moderated.
"What's our liability exposure?"
The server is peer-run mutual aid — not an agency program, endorsement, or official channel. Respite arrangements still require licensing-worker approval; the server only helps families find each other faster.
"Who is actually in the server?"
Invite-only. Membership is verified by moderators before roles are granted, and roles identify region and status.
"Our workers don't have time for another platform."
They don't need any time. Most of the value — peer answers, item exchange, resource referrals — happens without worker involvement. Participation is optional; visibility is available.
"Bad information will spread."
#resources is curated by moderators. Inaccurate guidance is corrected or removed, and a department liaison can post authoritative corrections whenever warranted.

04 What we are — and aren't — asking

What we're asking

  • Recognize the server as a legitimate peer-support resource for licensed families.
  • Permit workers to pass the invite link to licensed families — as information, not endorsement.
  • Optionally, name a liaison who can post announcements to a region's families in one place.

What we're not asking

  • Funding, staffing, or policy changes of any kind.
  • Caseworker time, monitoring duties, or moderation responsibilities.
  • Endorsement of peer respite arrangements — or liability for them.
  • Access to case information. The server is built so none is ever needed.

05 A note for caseworkers specifically

You can hand an overwhelmed new foster parent a community instead of just another phone number. One post in a region's channels reaches every family there at once — no more relayed messages.

And if you ever want to see what families in your region actually need — the real gaps, not the filtered version — an observer role will show you. Lurking is a perfectly good way to participate.

Every family that stays is a placement that doesn't move — and every placement that doesn't move is a child with one less loss. We'd like your help getting the invite into the right hands.
