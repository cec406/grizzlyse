Field notes / Deployment

Deployment playbook

My starting point for taking a system from the first conversation to a rollout people can use and support. These are working practices and example checks, not a record of a client engagement.

Read the playbookBack to selected work

01 / Delivery workflow

Start with what the operator needs to do.

The first deliverable isn't a stack diagram. It's agreement on the workflow, constraints, and what must be true for the rollout to count as successful.

  1. 01 / Discover

    Watch someone do the job

    Walk through a real task with the person who does it. Note where data enters, who's responsible for the next step, and what happens when the normal process breaks.

  2. 02 / Design

    Make the constraints explicit

    Write down who needs access, where the system will run, which tools it must work with, and how much downtime is acceptable. Use those constraints to choose what to build and what to reuse.

  3. 03 / Deploy

    Agree on go-live and rollback criteria

    Test the workflows that matter, confirm backups and recovery steps, and identify who can call off the cutover. After deployment, check the system from an operator's device, not just from the host.

  4. 04 / Support

    Hand over more than a URL

    Leave a runbook that covers routine tasks, access, updates, and recovery. Walk through it with the owner and agree on how to report and escalate issues.

02 / Worked example

What would I check before a shop dashboard goes live?

The GrizzWorks demo makes the workflow easy to review. A real rollout would also need to answer questions about devices, access, and ownership.

Verify the workflow

Have someone create and update a work order from the shop floor. Confirm the office sees the change and the wall display shows only what it should.

Agree on a fallback

Decide what the crew uses if the new system is unavailable. Test a restore with sample data before the shop depends on it.

Make ownership clear

Record who maintains the host, where backups go, how staff report problems, and who approves the next update.
